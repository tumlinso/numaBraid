# 2026-08-29 custom relay results

## Outcome

The selected setting is a two-buffer ring with 12 MiB chunks. At 256 MiB it
achieved 5.166 GB/s average across the four directed diagonals in the refined
sweep and 5.166 GB/s again in the final full-size campaign (5.136-5.196 GB/s by
direction). This is 99.7% of the independently measured 5.178 GB/s same-NUMA
PCIe P2P bottleneck and 3.3% faster than the 5.000 GB/s whole-buffer relay.

The exact ratio uses the NB-02 SM-pull PCIe measurement as the available direct
physical-leg baseline. Small differences above an individual earlier leg result
are measurement-method and run variance, not evidence that the relay exceeds a
physical limit.

## Size response

The final selected configuration averaged approximately 0.298 GB/s at 4 KiB,
2.23 GB/s at 64 KiB, 4.64 GB/s at 1 MiB, 5.10 GB/s at 16 MiB, and 5.17 GB/s at
256 MiB. The custom path is a throughput path; launch, event, and two-hop copy
latency dominate small messages.

## Tuning interpretation

- One 16 MiB staging buffer achieved 4.972 GB/s; two achieved 5.160 GB/s. This
  controlled comparison demonstrates useful PCIe/NVLink overlap.
- A third or fourth buffer did not materially improve throughput. Two buffers
  are retained as the smallest effective ring.
- 256 KiB chunks fell to about 4.77 GB/s because host scheduling, event, and copy
  setup overhead became material. Chunks from 8-25 MiB formed a broad plateau.
- Simultaneous reverse directions retained about 5.13 GB/s per direction and
  reached 10.26 GB/s aggregate, so this tested full-duplex case introduced only
  about 0.7% per-direction loss relative to the unidirectional mean.
- The Nsight Systems trace contains 176 peer-copy records for each of the eight
  directed physical legs. PCIe chunks averaged 2.34-2.37 ms and carrier NVLink
  chunks 87.5-88.6 microseconds. Interval joins in the exported SQLite trace
  show 98.28-98.29% of egress copy time overlapped an ingress copy.

## Route and correctness

Every payload transfer is explicitly issued as source-to-relay CUDA P2P followed
by relay-to-destination CUDA P2P. The route constructor verifies stable PCI
identity, same-NUMA source/relay locality, active relay/destination NVLink,
directed peer access, V100 identity, and absence of direct diagonal peer access.
There is no host-staged fallback. Wrong topology, wrong pointer ownership,
capacity overflow, and staging reuse while a transfer is active are explicit
errors.

Hardware correctness covered all four directed diagonals and sizes 0, 1, 31,
4095, 4096, 4097, 65521, 1048573, 16777219, and 67108871 bytes. Pure unit tests
cover rejected NUMA, NVLink, peer-access, and direct-path observations.
